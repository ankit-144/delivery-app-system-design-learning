# 6.2 — Timeouts, circuit breaker, bulkhead

> **Phase 6: notifications, from a sync call to Kafka.** Three standard protections for a caller, and
> the question none of them answers.

## Run it

Same chain and 2 s vendor as 6.1, at 30 orders/s:

```bash
make load ID=6.2 MODE=broken    # no protection
make load ID=6.2 MODE=timeout   # notify-timeout-ms=300
make load ID=6.2 MODE=fixed     # timeout + circuit breaker + bulkhead (max 10 calls in flight)
```

| Mode | Order p50 | Order p95 | SMS core counted as sent | …as dropped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no protection | 2,040 ms | 2,063 ms | 1,583 | 0 |
| timeout 300 ms | 309 ms | 331 ms | 0 | **1,414** |
| timeout + breaker + bulkhead | **7 ms** | 308 ms | 0 | **1,474** |

## The three protections

All three are in [`NotificationClient`](../../services/core-service/src/main/java/dev/quickdrop/core/notification/NotificationClient.java).

**Timeout**: stop waiting after 300 ms. A thread is held for at most 300 ms, so orders take ~309 ms.

**Circuit breaker** (Resilience4j): after most of the last 20 calls failed or were slow, stop calling
for 5 s and fail instantly. Then let 3 trial calls through and close again if they work. That's why
orders dropped to **7 ms**: most calls never left core. The 300 ms p95 is the trial calls.

```
 CLOSED ──(≥50% failed, or ≥80% slower than 1 s)──▶ OPEN ──(5 s)──▶ HALF_OPEN ──(3 calls ok)──▶ CLOSED
```

**Bulkhead**: at most 10 calls to notification-service at once; the 11th fails immediately instead of
waiting. Named after ship compartments: a leak floods one compartment, not the whole ship. Even if
notification-service hangs, it can tie up at most 10 of core's threads. Browsing and checkout keep
the other 40.

## The catch: "dropped" isn't "not delivered"

Core counted 1,414 SMS as dropped in the timeout run. We checked the vendor afterwards
(`curl localhost:8090/v1/stats`, `GET /admin/reconcile/sms`):

```
core:     0 sent, 701 dropped
vendor:   601 accepted
reconcile: {"ordersPlaced": 601, "orderSmsReceivedByVendor": 601, "ordersWithoutSms": 0}
```

**Every SMS was delivered.** Core stopped *waiting* after 300 ms, but notification-service kept working
and the vendor answered 2 s later. A timeout tells you **nothing** about whether the other side did the
work.

That matters the moment you add retries: retry on timeout and every customer gets **two** SMS (and with
payments, two charges). The fix for that is idempotency: the same `messageId` on every attempt, and a
receiver that recognises repeats (1.3, K3).

With the breaker open, though, messages really are dropped: they never leave core. The customer
quietly gets no confirmation. The protections save the order and lose the SMS. Phase 6 continues with: *don't
drop it, store it* (6.3).

## Choosing values

- **Timeout**: from the dependency's normal latency (p99 + margin), not a round number. Check it against your
  own latency budget (2.7).
- **Breaker**: thresholds tuned so a few failures don't trip it, and a dead dependency trips it within seconds.
  Alert on breaker state changes (**Redis & scale-out / Kafka** dashboards show breaker metrics).
- **Bulkhead size**: Little's law. Expected rate × normal latency × headroom.
