# 6.8 — An OTP stuck behind a promo blast

> **Phase 6: notifications, from a sync call to Kafka.** One topic means one line. Urgent and bulk
> messages need different lines.

## Story

A 40,000-promo blast is queued; at the vendor's pace it takes minutes to drain. Meanwhile people try to log in:
2 OTP requests per second. An OTP is useless after a minute or two, because people give up and churn.

## Run it

```bash
make load ID=6.8 MODE=broken    # otp-topic=shared
make load ID=6.8 MODE=fixed     # otp-topic=priority
```

| | OTPs requested (60 s) | Delivered during the run | Delivery delay p95 |
|---|---|---|---|
| shared topic | ~120 | **0** | — (still waiting behind the promos) |
| priority topic | ~120 | **130** | **0.06 s** |

(We also hit this one by accident. A leftover promo backlog from 6.5 delayed every **order confirmation** in our first
run of 6.6, and the reconciliation said every single order was missing its SMS.)

## Why

Kafka keeps order **within a partition**. An OTP lands at the end of its partition, behind thousands of promos that
arrived first, and consumers process strictly in order. A message's wait = the backlog in front of it ÷ consumption
rate. "Urgent" isn't a concept Kafka has.

## Fix: separate lines

```
 OTP    ──▶ topic notifications-priority (3 partitions) ──▶ group notification-service-priority  (own consumers, own lag)
 others ──▶ topic notifications          (6 partitions) ──▶ group notification-service
```

`otp-topic=priority` makes core publish OTPs to their own topic
([`KafkaPublisher.topicFor`](../../services/core-service/src/main/java/dev/quickdrop/core/notification/KafkaPublisher.java)).
notification-service reads it with a separate consumer group
([`KafkaConsumers`](../../services/notification-service/src/main/java/dev/quickdrop/notification/KafkaConsumers.java)).
Its lag stays at zero however big the promo backlog grows. **Kafka & notifications → Consumer lag** shows the two
lines side by side, and **Delivery delay by type** shows OTPs at ~50 ms.

## Variations

- **One topic per kind of work, sized for its own traffic** (transactional / marketing / bulk exports).
- **Dedicated capacity at the shared bottleneck.** Separate topics don't help if both lines then hit the same vendor
  rate limit (6.7). Reserve part of the vendor budget for OTPs, or use a separate vendor account.
- **Expire what's too old**: an OTP older than 5 minutes should be dropped, not sent.
- **Bulk sends at low priority by design**: marketing blasts go through a throttled line.

## Discussion

1. Why doesn't adding more consumers to the shared topic fix OTP latency during a blast?
2. Which other messages in a delivery app deserve their own line?
3. The priority line and the promo line share the vendor's 100/s limit. How would you make sure OTPs always get through?
