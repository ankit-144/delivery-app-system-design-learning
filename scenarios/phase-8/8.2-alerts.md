# 8.2 — Alerts: which ones should wake someone up?

> **Phase 8: operating it.** Page on what customers feel (symptoms), not on what machines feel
> (causes). Measure "what customers feel" against an SLO, and alert on how fast you're burning its budget.

## Run it

```bash
make up PHASE=8
make load ID=8.2          # 7 minutes, plus 1 minute for alerts to resolve
```

Normal traffic (30 checkouts/s, 50 browses/s) with two incidents:

- **t=60–150 s**: a nightly export job pegs notification-1's CPU (`/admin/chaos/cpu`) while marketing blasts 30,000
  promos through Kafka. Checkout never waits for notification-service.
- **t=150–330 s**: core-2 fails 75% of its checkouts (a bad deploy on one of three instances): about 25% of all
  checkouts fail.

The script prints what reached the "pager" (Alertmanager → webhook in notification-service):

```
 t= 137 s  FIRING    HighCpu                      [warning] notification-1 is using 89% of its CPU limit
 t= 167 s  RESOLVED  HighCpu                      [warning] notification-1 is using 92% of its CPU limit
 t= 189 s  FIRING    CheckoutErrorBudgetSlowBurn  [ticket]  Checkout errors at 5.95%: slowly burning the error budget
 t= 224 s  FIRING    CheckoutErrorBudgetFastBurn  [page]    Checkouts are failing: 7.35% errors over the last minute
 t= 404 s  RESOLVED  CheckoutErrorBudgetFastBurn  [page]    Checkouts are failing: 15.34% errors over the last minute
 (plus a Watchdog heartbeat every minute)
```

| Incident | Customers affected? | What fired | Right call? |
|---|---|---|---|
| export job + promo blast | no: checkout p99 stayed at 17 ms all run | `HighCpu` warning | it would have woken someone for nothing |
| bad deploy on core-2 | yes: ~25% of checkouts failed for 3 minutes | slow-burn ticket after 39 s, **page after 74 s**, resolved 74 s after the fix | yes |

No cause alert noticed the bad deploy: it used no extra CPU, filled no pool and took no instance down. Two details
worth knowing: a *resolved* notification repeats the annotations from when it last fired (hence "15% errors" in a
message that means "it's over"), and the slow-burn ticket keeps firing for a few more minutes because its short
window is 3 minutes.

## Symptoms page, causes don't

[`alerts.yml`](../../infra/prometheus/rules/alerts.yml) has two groups on purpose:

| | Examples | Fires when | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **symptoms** | checkout errors vs SLO, checkout p99, OTPs delayed, notification backlog growing | customers are affected | **paging** |
| **causes** | CPU > 80%, DB pool waiting, instance down | a machine is busy or gone | dashboards, diagnosis, tickets |

A cause alert has two problems. It fires when nobody is hurt (the blast: CPU at 100% is the system doing its job),
which trains people to ignore pages. And it's silent for problems nobody predicted: the bad deploy used no extra CPU
and filled no pool, and no cause alert fired. Every incident that matters eventually shows up as a symptom.

## SLOs and burn rates

**SLO**: 99.5% of `POST /api/orders` don't fail with a 5xx, over 30 days. The other 0.5% is the **error
budget**: about 3.6 hours of total outage a month, or a steady trickle of failures, to spend on deploys,
experiments and bad luck.

**Burn rate** = (current error rate) ÷ (allowed error rate). Burn rate 1 spends the budget exactly in 30 days.
Burn rate 14.4 spends it in about 2 days.

**Multiwindow, multi-burn-rate alerts** (Google SRE workbook):

| Alert | Condition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| fast burn → page | burn > 14.4 over 1 h **and** over 5 m | a real outage: 2% of the monthly budget per hour |
| slow burn → ticket | burn > 6 over 6 h **and** over 30 m | a chronic problem: look at it this week |

The long window proves it's not a blip. The short window makes the alert resolve within minutes of the fix,
rather than an hour later. In this lab every window is divided by 12 (1 h → 5 m, 5 m → 1 m, 6 h → 30 m,
30 m → 3 m) so a 7-minute run behaves like hours of production.

**Operating (Phase 8) → Checkout error ratio vs the SLO** shows both windows against the thresholds. Watch the 5-minute
line climb slowly while the 1-minute line jumps: the alert waits for the slow one.

## The pipeline

```
 Prometheus  ──evaluates rules every 5 s──▶  Alertmanager  ──groups, dedupes, routes──▶  webhook (notification-service)
 (alerts.yml)                                 (alertmanager.yml)                         GET /api/alerts
```

- **Grouping**: alerts with the same `alertname` fire together become one notification (`group_wait: 10s`).
- **Inhibition**: while a `severity=page` alert fires, `kind=cause` warnings are muted. They're probably the reason,
  and the on-call engineer is already looking.
- **Watchdog**: an alert that always fires. If the receiver stops hearing it every minute, the alerting pipeline is
  broken. Production setups send it to an external "dead man's switch" service.
- **Who watches the watchers?** The webhook is notification-1. Try `docker pause qd-notification-1` during an
  incident: the page never arrives, and Alertmanager's `alertmanager_notifications_failed_total` climbs. Paging
  through the system you're monitoring means an outage can also take down its own alarm.
